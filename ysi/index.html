Why the bigger numbers, and why I can actually deliver them

Options 2–4 aren't guesses. Each one leans on a problem I've already solved end to end, for real, paying clients — not a plan, a working thing:

  • The AI guide itself — built, live, and paid for by a psychology center in Russia. Same engine, English version, try it yourself: aka-gst.ru/orion-case-en. Getting a guide to answer correctly, refuse safely, and hand off to a human when it should — that's most of the hard part of Options 1–3, and it's done.
  • A pleasant, non-robotic voice — the version live on that same psychologist's site is a properly rendered voice, not the free browser voice you hear in this demo (that one's a placeholder — a browser's built-in voice, fine for a quick demo, not what ships). Matching that quality in English for meditation needs real GPU render time, which is why Meditation Voice Studio is priced the way it is.
  • A real practice diary with real users — a Buddhist meditation/yoga diary bot has been live for over a week, six daily prompts, bilingual, with export and full data deletion. Not a prototype; people are actually using it.

The risk in a project like this is usually "will the AI actually work correctly and safely." That part is already answered — what's left is fitting it to YSI's real content and scale, which is exactly what the pilot buys.
